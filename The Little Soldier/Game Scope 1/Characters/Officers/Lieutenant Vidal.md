## Overview
Company officer. Ambitious but distant. Thinks about promotion, not his men. Studies maps by candlelight. His role is institutional — he represents the chain of command above Morin's practical authority.

## Appearance
- Studying a map by candlelight
- Acknowledges you with a nod but doesn't look up
- "His face goes blank" reading the withdrawal dispatch
- Sword drawn at the battle opening

## Background
- Career officer in the Army of Italy
- Focused on tactics and strategy — the passes, the terrain, the enemy dispositions
- Not cruel, just preoccupied — his men are abstractions in a larger picture
- Ambitious — wants to be noticed by the right people

## Personality
- Distant — acknowledges with a nod, doesn't engage
- Analytical — mutters about passes and approaches
- Professional — "I need facts, not nerves"
- Capable of recognition: "Good eyes, soldier" / "The 14th held together on the march. That matters."
- Values competence over bravery

## Arc
Distant authority figure who bookends the battle: gives the order to fight, reads the order to retreat, acknowledges the march at the end. He does not change. He represents the institutional army above the intimate world of the section.

## Relationships
- **Player**: Starts at 20. Distant professional. Approval comes through demonstrated competence (reporting intelligence, reporting to unit).
- **Morin**: Chain of command. Vidal decides, Morin executes.
- **The Section**: His men are a resource, not a family. This isn't callousness — it's the officer's necessary distance.

## Design Notes
- Equivalent to Leclerc at Rivoli (company officer), but lower rank (Lieutenant vs Captain)
- Much less present than Leclerc — fewer spoken lines, less rallying
- Key interaction point: Smoke on the Heights event (only camp event where Vidal speaks directly)
- Base stats: Valor 45, Morale 80, maxMorale 100, Relationship 20
