Tier 1 — In the Ranks

Five visual approaches for the soldier's-eye battlefield view — Battle of Rivoli, 1797

Panel 1: Silhouette Ranks

High-contrast silhouettes. French soldiers as dark shapes seen from behind. The Austrian enemy is a pale mass in the distance. Player marked in gold.

Panel 2: Woodcut / Engraving

Period-illustration style with fine lines, cross-hatching, and stippling. Soldiers as line drawings. Player marked with warm amber strokes.

Panel 3: Fog of War

Atmospheric and minimal. Heavy smoke and haze obscure everything beyond arm's reach. The Austrians are barely visible. This is what battle looks like after the first volley.

Panel 4: Side Cross-Section

Literal side view of the formation. Three ranks deep: front rank kneeling, second rank presenting, third rank loading. Enemy column approaching from the right. Player marked in the second rank.

Panel 5: Silhouette Ranks v2

Bold, stylized shapes — shadow puppets, not anatomy. Dense foreground wall, mid-rank visible through gaps, Austrians resolving from haze. Player's own perspective — no marker.

6. Abstract Musket Line

No figurative soldiers. The experience of being in the ranks rendered as pure geometry: diagonal musket barrels crossing the frame, a forest of bayonet points, muzzle flash streaks, drifting smoke layers. Atmosphere through line-work and gradients, not anatomy.

7. The Blend

Panel 5's human presence meets Panel 6's geometric power. Foreground = massive dark shapes framing the view (barely recognizable as soldiers — just shoulders and hat brims). Middle rank = varied silhouettes with musket barrels extending as bold diagonal lines. Each soldier is genuinely different. Two muzzle flashes. Dawn warmth at the horizon. One thick musket barrel from OUR position crosses the lower frame.
