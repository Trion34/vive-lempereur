# Voltri Visual Reference — Public Domain Image Research

Research date: 15 March 2026

Images for the opening chapter of *The Little Soldier*, set in Voltri (a coastal town west of Genoa) where the French Army of Italy was garrisoned before the Battle of Voltri, 10 April 1796.

---

## 1. Battle Paintings & Engravings of Voltri

### 1.1 Giuseppe Pietro Bagetti — "Vue de Voltri le 10 avril 1796"

- **Artist:** Giuseppe Pietro Bagetti (1764-1831), Piedmontese painter/cartographer
- **Date:** c. 1801-1807
- **Medium:** Gouached watercolor (aquarelle gouachee), 53 x 81 cm
- **Current location:** Musee des Chateaux de Versailles et de Trianon
- **URLs:**
  - Louvre Collections: https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl020009522
  - Wikimedia Commons file: `Vue de Voltri, 10 avril 1796.jpg` (1917 x 1188 px)
  - Google Arts & Culture (artist page): https://artsandculture.google.com/entity/giuseppe-pietro-bagetti/m0_s23_4
- **License:** Public domain (artist died 1831). Wikimedia file available.
- **Description:** Panoramic battlefield view showing rolling hills meeting the sea near Voltri. The town's buildings cluster on the right side. Cannon smoke crowns the peaks on the left where combat is occurring. Shows General Cervoni's French force defending against Austrian attack. Drawn on-site by Bagetti, a military engineer-geographer who visited the actual locations.
- **Usefulness: HIGH** — This is THE primary image of Voltri in 1796. Shows the coastal topography, the town's position, and the landscape the player's character would inhabit. Bagetti was called "the prince of watercolors" and his battlefield views are topographically accurate.

### 1.2 Joseph Skelton (after Bagetti) — "Combat de Voltri, 9 avril 1796"

- **Artist:** Joseph Skelton (1785-1850), engraver; after Bagetti's original painting
- **Engravers:** Schoeder and Misbach
- **Date:** 19th century
- **Medium:** Engraving, 33 x 48 cm
- **Current location:** Palais Fesch, Cabinet des arts graphiques, Ajaccio, France (MNA 954.1.129)
- **URL:** https://www.musee-fesch.com/estampes/combat-de-voltri-9-avril-1796-322544
- **License:** Public domain (all creators died pre-1900). Museum collection.
- **Description:** Engraved reproduction of Bagetti's watercolor, depicting the same battle scene. The engraving format gives it a different texture — sharper lines, more dramatic contrast.
- **Usefulness: HIGH** — An engraving of the same Voltri battle scene. The black-and-white style could work well as game art reference for a parchment/ink aesthetic.

### 1.3 "Combat de Voltri" — Anonymous Steel Engraving (c. 1840)

- **Artist:** Anonymous
- **Date:** c. 1840
- **Medium:** Steel engraving, sheet ~41 x 29 cm, plate ~32 x 24 cm
- **Source:** Listed on ZVAB (antiquarian book dealer)
- **URL:** https://www.zvab.com/kunst-grafik-poster/Combat-Voltri-Genova-veduta-incisione-Ansicht/22573290022/bd

- **Inscription:** "Genes 1834" (lower right)

## 5. Ligurian Coast — Period Paintings

### 5.1 Antonio Travi — Ligurian Coastal Scenes (c. 1640-1660)

- **Artist:** Antonio Travi, called "Le Sestri" (1608-1665)
- **Works:** "Sea View with Fishermen and Tumbledown Tower," "Coastal Landscape with Fishermen"
- **Medium:** Oil on canvas
- **URLs:**
  - WGA: https://www.wga.hu/html_m/t/travi/sea_view.html
  - WGA: https://www.wga.hu/html_m/t/travi/coastal.html
- **License:** Public domain (artist died 1665). Images available on Web Gallery of Art.
- **Description:** Travi was the first true painter of the Ligurian coast. Born in Sestri Ponente (a town adjacent to Voltri), his paintings show the actual shoreline near Genoa — fishing scenes, ruined towers, small boats, rocky coasts. He filled foregrounds with fishing activity and towing scenes.
- **Usefulness: HIGH** — Travi painted the exact stretch of coast where Voltri sits. Sestri Ponente is the next town east. These paintings show the authentic character of the Ligurian shore: fishermen, small harbors, rocky coasts, ruined structures. The landscape would have looked very similar 130 years later in 1796.

### 5.2 J.M.W. Turner — "View on the Ligurian Coast near Genoa"

## Notes on Voltri's Visual Character (from research)

For composition reference, here is what Voltri looks like as a setting:

- **Geography:** Voltri sits at the apex of the Ligurian arc, where the coast turns. It marks the division point between the western Ponente Riviera and the eastern Levante Riviera. 16 km west of central Genoa.
- **Coastline:** Grey sand and pebble beaches, rocky areas. Two main beaches flanking the town center, named after the churches nearby.
- **Churches:** Chiesa dei Santi Nicolo ed Erasmo (patron saint of sailors, 13th century origin, enlarged 17th century with Baroque dome completed 1714). Chiesa di Sant'Ambrogio with prominent campanile.
- **Architecture:** Low-rise 19th-century buildings in Ligurian style, narrow streets, sea visible between buildings.
- **Industry:** Paper production was the historic main activity (Middle Ages onward).
- **Setting in 1796:** A small fishing and paper-making town on the coast, with church towers visible from the sea, hills rising behind, narrow streets opening to harbor views. The French Army of Italy was poorly supplied — soldiers lacked uniforms and shoes, reduced to looting. The garrison at Voltri would have been a ragged, hungry force in a picturesque coastal setting.
