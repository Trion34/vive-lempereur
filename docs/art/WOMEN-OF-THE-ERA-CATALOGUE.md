# Women of the Era Art Catalogue -- The Little Soldier

Public domain artwork depicting women of the French Revolutionary / Napoleonic era (1790-1810) for character design reference. Focus on the Italian Campaign 1796-97 setting.

**Research date**: 2026-03-15
**Method**: Systematic search across Wikimedia Commons, Metropolitan Museum, Rijksmuseum, Art Institute of Chicago, National Gallery of Art, Wellcome Collection, NYPL, and other public domain sources.

---

## How to Use This Catalogue

- **Commons page URL** = Wikimedia file description page with full metadata and license
- **Museum page URL** = official collection page with provenance, description, high-res download
- **Direct image URL** = the actual image file (for preview)
- **License**: "Public domain" means unrestricted use. Museum "Open Access / CC0" means the same.
- Entries marked **HIGH** usefulness are directly relevant to specific game characters or types.
- Categories: `portrait`, `fashion-plate`, `genre-scene`, `camp-follower`, `italian-woman`, `sculpture`

## PORTRAITS OF SPECIFIC WOMEN

### 1. Empress Josephine in the Park at Malmaison
- **Artist**: Pierre-Paul Prud'hon (1758-1823)
- **Date**: c. 1805-1809
- **Medium**: Oil on canvas
- **Location**: Musee du Louvre, Paris (Denon wing, Room 702)
- **Commons page**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:The_Empress_Josephine_by_Pierre-Paul_Prud%27hon
- **Direct image**: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/6/69/Josephine_par_Prud%27hon%2C_malmaison.jpg
- **License**: Public domain
- **Category**: portrait
- **Description**: The most celebrated image of Josephine. She sits alone on moss-covered rocks in the gardens of Malmaison, wearing a white dress embroidered with gold, red cashmere shawl, tiaras in her hair. An idealised, melancholy figure lost in thought, far from the pomp of court.
- **Relevance**: Napoleon's wife, married March 1796 just before the Italian Campaign. Quintessential Directoire beauty. Could inspire the archetype of an aristocratic NPC or a letter/locket portrait.
- **Usefulness**: HIGH

### 2. Josephine de Beauharnais -- Gerard (Hermitage version)
- **Artist**: Francois Gerard (1770-1837)
- **Date**: c. 1801
- **Medium**: Oil on canvas
- **Location**: Hermitage Museum, St. Petersburg
- **Commons page**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Madame_Bonaparte_at_Malmaison_(G%C3%A9rard)
- **License**: Public domain
- **Description**: Half-length portrait of Josephine as "Madame Bonaparte" at Malmaison. Simpler, more intimate than the later coronation portraits. White Directoire dress, dark curled hair, gentle expression.
- **Relevance**: Shows Josephine closer to the 1796-97 period, before becoming Empress. More useful for the game's timeframe than the coronation portraits.
- **Usefulness**: HIGH

### 3. Portrait of Madame Recamier -- David (Louvre)
- **Artist**: Jacques-Louis David (1748-1825)
- **Date**: 1800
- **Medium**: Oil on canvas, 174 x 244 cm
- **Location**: Musee du Louvre, Paris (INV 3708)
- **Commons page**: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Madame_R%C3%A9camier_-_Jacques-Louis_David_-_Louvre_INV_3708
- **Direct image**: https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/5/5f/Madame_R%C3%A9camier_painted_by_Jacques-Louis_David_in_1800.jpg
- **License**: Public domain
- **Description**: The iconic unfinished portrait. Juliette Recamier reclines on a Directoire meridienne sofa in a simple white Empire-line dress, bare arms, short hair "a la Titus". Stripped-down neoclassical composition with almost no background detail. The epitome of Directoire elegance.
- **Relevance**: THE reference image for Directoire women's fashion and beauty ideals. Defines the look of the era. Essential for any female NPC design -- the dress, the hairstyle, the furniture, the pose.
- **Usefulness**: HIGH

### 17. Portrait of Madeleine -- Benoist
- **Artist**: Marie-Guillemine Benoist (1768-1826)
- **Date**: 1800
- **Medium**: Oil on canvas, 81 x 65 cm
- **Location**: Musee du Louvre, Paris
- **Museum page**: https://collections.louvre.fr/en/ark:/53355/cl010065532
- **License**: Public domain
- **Category**: portrait
- **Description**: A woman of African descent named Madeleine, who came from Guadeloupe after slavery was abolished in 1794. ## Known Gaps

- **Italian peasant women (contadina)** -- Very few public domain paintings of ordinary Italian women from the 1790s. Most Italian women in art are painted as classical goddesses or saints.
- **Actual vivandiere from the 1790s** -- Most vivandiere images are from the 1820s-1860s (Bellange, Charlet, Lalaisse), retrospectively depicting Napoleonic era. No confirmed 1796-97 era depiction found.
- **Women of Piedmont/Northern Italy specifically** -- While Lady Hamilton covers Naples, the game is set in Piedmont/Lombardy where far fewer portraits survive.
- **Camp scenes with women** -- Swebach and others painted camp scenes, but locating specific public domain digital files of these proved difficult. The V&A blog and PICRYL are the best leads.
- **French working-class women of the 1790s** -- Greuze is 1761 (pre-Revolution), Daumier is 1860s. The gap between is poorly covered in easily-accessible public domain collections.
